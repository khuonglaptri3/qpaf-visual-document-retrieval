# M5.5 — Research basis and interpretation boundaries

Reviewed 2026-10-27. The adopted M5.1 experiment, rather than a new search over
methods, controls what is run. R5-01–03 are validation diagnostics; R5-04 is
deferred on this machine at the user's instruction.

## What the literature informs

[Louis, van Dijck and Spanakis, COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.290/)
study hybrid retrieval in French legal search. They report that fusion can
help zero-shot systems yet hurt stronger in-domain systems unless weights are
carefully calibrated. Appendix A describes per-query min-max and z-score
normalization. This motivates checking channel contribution and score scale;
their dataset results do not predict the outcome on ViDoSeek.

[OpenSearch normalization documentation](https://docs.opensearch.org/latest/search-plugins/search-pipelines/normalization-processor/)
illustrates that the same raw scores can yield different fusion rankings under
different normalizers. Its product-specific z-score behavior uses sample
standard deviation and clips nonpositive values. Those details do not match
M5.1: this experiment must use population standard deviation and retain
negative z-scores, mapping only a constant channel to zero. Do not copy that
product implementation as the study's scoring contract.

[PyTorch 2.7 CUDA semantics](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.7/notes/cuda.html#asynchronous-execution)
explains why asynchronous device work needs synchronization or CUDA events
for timing. A CPU run's elapsed analysis time cannot establish CUDA latency.
[PyTorch peak-memory documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.7/generated/torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated.html)
defines peak tensor allocation and the reset operation; it is distinct from
reserved device memory, process RSS and container RAM.

## Repository evidence that matters

- M4.4/M4.5: train-fitted static `[0, 0.1, 0.9]` achieves validation nDCG@10
  `0.8714141246842781`; ColQwen alone `0.8692955672259587`; uniform min-max
  CombSUM `0.8412541217549685`. An equal mixture is not established as beneficial.
- M5.1 already locks the eight CPU rows. Removing a scoring channel does not
  remove its previously retrieved pages from the frozen candidate union.
- Min-max and z-score are applied independently per query and per channel.
  A singleton's within-channel rank is preserved by positive affine scaling;
  changes in fused rankings reflect relative channel influence.
- Static weights and QPAF checkpoints stay fixed. A diagnostic row with a
  higher validation point estimate is exploratory evidence, not a replacement
  primary baseline or a held-out improvement claim.
- M3.6 used label-free train/test inputs to meet its distinct-query workload.
  M5.5's validation-only roster does not inherit that access permission.
  The metric policy says >=1,000 measured queries; M3.6 specifically used
  distinct IDs. Repeating 171 validation queries cannot create 1,000 distinct
  IDs. Any new counting interpretation for validation-only timing needs an
  explicit measurement protocol; the base policy does not spell out that
  interpretation by itself.

## Questions the report must answer

Does removing BM25, BGE or visual scores raise or lower uniform fusion quality
on the same rows? Does changing only score normalization change that result?
Are effects spread across queries or concentrated? Which measurements were
actually recomputed here, and which GPU measurements remain NOT_RUN?

These diagnostics can guide a separately designed future method study. They
do not reopen the frozen QPAF-v1 method or TEST-172 selection rules.
